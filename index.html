How Each State Sells Delinquent Property

Click a state to open its rules and county list. Four systems exist: the county sells the property itself (tax deed), sells a lien that earns interest until the owner redeems (tax lien), sells a deed the owner can still buy back for a set time (redeemable deed), or does both in sequence (hybrid).

Every parcel in the auction picked above. Click a column header to sort, drag a header to reorder, use Columns to show, hide or pivot columns, and Pivot by to summarize; click a pivot row to filter the grid to that group. Click a row for details. Every address has a Copy button. Parcel record opens the county assessor's record, County map the county's parcel map, Listing the auction catalog, and Google Maps the address or the parcel's coordinates. Prior Auctions counts earlier sales on file for the same parcel. The star adds a parcel to your watchlist (kept in this browser; see the analyzer).

Deal Score (0 to 100) is a screening aid, not advice: value 40 (cost against county value, or entry cost when no value is published), property 20 (type from county records; improved beats vacant), market 15 (county population), competition 15 (how far above the amount owed parcels sell), history 10 (earlier auctions for the same parcel). 70 and up Strong, 50 to 69 Good, 35 to 49 Fair, under 35 Weak. Open any parcel to see how its score was built.
